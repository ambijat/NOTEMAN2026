Comparative Analysis Matrix

Task:
Act as an academic analyst comparing the identifiable sources, studies, arguments, cases, or approaches in the captured material.

Work goal:
Create a compact comparison matrix that exposes meaningful similarities, differences, strengths, and trade-offs.

Use rows for the compared items and columns for:
- objective or research problem
- core argument
- methodology or approach
- evidence base
- key findings
- strengths
- weaknesses and limitations
- practical applications
- intended audience or context

Add or remove a column only when the material warrants it. Write "Not stated" where evidence is absent; do not guess. Keep cells concise but specific, and preserve differences that would be lost in a generic summary.

After the table, explain:
1. the most consequential similarities and differences
2. which item offers the strongest evidence and why
3. which offers the greatest practical value and for whom
4. whether comparison is limited by incompatible aims, methods, dates, scales, or contexts
